Sports comedy · Feature

BADDY DADDY

written by
Rhea Dudani

The high-intensity world of professional badminton is relentless, competitive, and elite. When a single dad—and worldwide badminton champion—gets stuck with his teenage daughter for the summer of the Thai Badminton Open, he must decide whether to follow his dreams or sacrifice everything to save his daughter from her bad-boy Thai boyfriend.

About

People tell Rhea she looks young for her age. Then they can’t believe how much shit she’s already done: an Oscar-nominated documentary, Sundance and Tribeca premieres, and working on (and winning) a presidential election. It’s all part of a lifelong quest for validation she’ll never get. Her Indian immigrant parents wanted an engineer, so naturally, she dropped out of engineering school to make films. Her family is thrilled.

After NYU Tisch came five years in production across politics, documentary and narrative. At AL Media, Thematic Campaigns and the DNC, she has made political ads for candidates like Joe Biden, Nithya Raman and Abdul El-Sayed. She swore 2020 would be her last cycle of political media. She has worked every cycle since.

Rhea wrote and directed the award-winning shorts Flowers and Bereft, and wrote a Nike commercial, which she considers her athletic career—she needs an inhaler to walk .1 miles. Her feature directorial debut, the documentary Jazba, is still in the works. Independent film is going great.

Now an Annenberg Fellow in the M.F.A. Screenwriting program at USC, she arrived thinking that with all the death, divorce and depression she knew about, she’d for sure be writing drama. Turns out she’s really funny. Or rather, she thinks she’s really funny. Other people look at each other when she tries to make a joke. Her scripts are about people who crave validation and will do anything to get it.

Her family has requested that all script sales go toward reimbursing them for engineering school.
